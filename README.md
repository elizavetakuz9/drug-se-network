# Drug Networks: Indications and Side Effects

This project builds and compares two networks over the same 118 approved nervous-system (ATC class N) medications: one linking drugs that treat the same conditions, the other linking drugs that cause the same adverse effects. The question is whether a drug's side-effect profile alone carries enough signal to recover its pharmacological class. Data comes from SIDER (drugs and reported adverse effects with frequencies) and repoDB (drugs and approved indications).

## Networks

- **Drug–Indication** - bipartite, 250 nodes, 352 edges
- **Drug–Side effect** - bipartite, weighted by reported frequency, 1,901 nodes, 17,304 edges
- **Drug–Drug by indication** - weighted projection on shared indications
- **Drug–Drug by SE count** - weighted projection on shared side effects, near-complete (density 0.98)
- **Drug–Drug by SE Jaccard** - weighted Jaccard over frequency vectors, sparsified by weight threshold
- **Drug–Drug by SE cosine** - cosine similarity over frequency vectors, sparsified by disparity filter

The two side-effect projections need sparsification because the raw count projection connects almost every pair of drugs. The disparity filter (Serrano et al., 2009) tests each edge against a null model of uniformly distributed node strength, preserving structure across scales rather than imposing a single global cut.

## Pipeline

1. Merge SIDER drug names, ATC codes and side-effect frequencies, keep MedDRA Preferred Terms, drop placebo rows
2. Filter to ATC class N, match against approved repoDB indications on normalized drug name
3. Resolve frequency placeholders and aggregate duplicate drug-SE pairs by maximum reported frequency
4. Build the bipartite networks and the four drug–drug projections
5. Describe topology: components, clustering, degree and edge-weight distributions, degree assortativity
6. Rank drugs by degree, strength, closeness, betweenness and PageRank, with bipartite normalization where needed
7. Sparsify the side-effect projections by weight threshold and by disparity filter
8. Detect communities with Leiden/Louvain, Infomap, edge betweenness and exact optimal modularity, compare by modularity, normalized cut and NMI
9. Test community composition and attribute assortativity against ATC levels 2-4
10. Score repositioning candidates against a high-burden anchor drug

## Main results

Indication similarity tracks pharmacological class closely, 58.7% of edges connect drugs of the same ATC2 class, with attribute assortativity of +0.64 at the finer ATC3 level, while side-effect similarity does not, dropping to 28% within-class and near-neutral assortativity that decreases as class granularity rises. 
The two networks also have almost disjoint sets of central drugs: broad-use agents like caffeine lead the indication network, while risperidone and tapentadol lead the side-effect network through the extent and typicality of their adverse profiles. Scoring alternatives to risperidone by shared indications, lower side-effect burden and dissimilar profile ranks aripiprazole first, a substitution independently supported in the clinical literature.
